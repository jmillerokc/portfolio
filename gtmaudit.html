Inquiry volume was never the issue. Every channel, every market, every campaign fed the top of the funnel without much trouble.

FIG. 01 / LEAD-TO-CLOSE FUNNEL
Only 14% of inquiries ever reach a scheduled consultation
Where To Focus
Consultation Scheduled to Qualification Completed drops 71%, the steepest stage-over-stage falloff in the funnel. Everything downstream is starved by what happens right there.

Improving the inquiry-to-consultation rate would lift the whole funnel, but the sharper diagnostic question was what happens between a scheduled consultation and a completed qualification. That gap could be a fit problem, a follow-up problem, or a scoring problem. It could not be answered without first fixing what the CRM was and was not capturing, which is where the audit went next.

02 / Diagnosis

The cheapest channels by lead were the most expensive by outcome.

Six paid channels, six very different stories once cost was measured against qualified leads instead of raw volume.

Channel
benchmarks

Every channel looked defensible in isolation. Side by side against qualification rate, the picture changed. Two channels were quietly carrying the program. Two more were consuming budget that looked efficient on the surface.

WINNER

Branded Search

41% leads to qualified · $480 cost per qualified lead. Most efficient channel in the mix. Intent is obvious. Protect and maintain.

SCALE

Competitor Conquest Search

48% leads to qualified. Highest qualification rate in the account. Test increased spend to grow volume.

MEASURE

Meta Lead Form

21% leads to qualified · spend not isolated in platform reporting. Converts well, but missing spend data to score true efficiency.

FIX

Video / Demand Gen

$1,920 cost per qualified lead. Highest cost per qualified lead in the mix. Spend isn't pulling weight as a prospecting engine. Shift toward awareness goals.

LOW QUALITY

Display

1.8% leads to qualified · $9 cost per lead. Cheap leads, but volume doesn't qualify. Reallocate.

LOW QUALITY

Automated Bidding / Broad Match

2.9% leads to qualified. Cheap leads, but volume doesn't qualify. Reallocate.

A blended $54 cost per lead against a $740 cost per qualified lead is the tell. That gap is driven mostly by the low-qualifying volume channels, and inflated further by a channel optimized for lead volume that isn't producing much of either quantity or quality.

None of this was fully visible without fixing the CRM first

Key Insight
A qualified-lead marker isn't clearly defined in the CRM. Outreach contacted leads at scale, and only 19% ever received a scheduled date, the largest measurable drop in the whole system.

The fix wasn't more spend or a new channel mix. It was date stamps on every lead status change, a qualification score built into the existing lead rating field, and status-reason picklists specific enough to tell a fit problem from a follow-up problem. Until that existed, every channel number above was a best estimate, not a measurement.

03 / Intervention

Spend and results were living in different states.

Overlaying dollars spent against qualified leads and closed business by state exposed a geography problem hiding under the channel problem.

Geo spend
alignment

Concentration Risk

6 states provided 33% of qualified leads but took 48% of spend.

Reallocating even a portion of the six overinvested states' budget toward the aligned and under-invested markets was the single highest-leverage move available, ahead of any channel-level fix. It also explained a pattern in the historical close data that had been sitting there the whole time.

Texas leads with 7 closed-won accounts. Georgia has 5, Florida and Ohio 4 each, Arizona and California 3 each, New Jersey and Arkansas 2 each. These eight states and eleven DMAs become the priority tier for the reallocated budget.

The Pattern
The states winning the most business were never the states getting the most budget. Alignment, not additional spend, was the lever the whole time.
